Vector Rally

You do not steer the car. You steer its acceleration.

How many are playing?

How it works
  • Your car stands on a corner of the paper, where two lines cross. It has a position and a velocity, both pairs of whole numbers.
  • Each turn you change the velocity by −1, 0 or +1 in each direction — nine choices — and the car then travels in a straight line by the whole new velocity. Neither part of it may leave −5…5.
  • Work out where that puts you. Nothing is marked on the track: adding the velocity to the position is the whole exercise. There is a switch on the race screen for showing the nine while the game is being explained.
  • The whole line from where you are to where you land has to stay on the track. At speed 5 it can cut the corner out of a bend with both of its ends still on the track — that counts as leaving the track, because it is.
  • Go off and the car stops just outside the edge, where it crossed it, with no speed left. From there it may only drive back on or stand still. The game shows exactly where the line left the track.
  • Cars are points. Two may not stand on the same point, and a move whose line goes exactly through another car is impossible — but passing close is not.
  • One lap. It counts when you cross the line the way the arrows point, having passed every hidden checkpoint in order. Following the arrows round is enough to collect them.

Taking a turn: press the crossing you have worked out. It is ringed while you hold it, with the speed the move would leave you with; let go to go there. Press the wrong one and nothing happens, and the game says which kind of wrong it was: Not reachable from your velocity means the sum was wrong, Occupied or Speed limit means the sum was right and the rules say no.

Moving the board: drag it. Zoom with the scroll wheel, a pinch, or the + and − buttons; ⟲ goes back to the car whose turn it is. The board never moves by itself.
