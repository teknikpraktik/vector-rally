Vector Rally

You do not steer the car. You steer its acceleration.

Track

Twelve tracks named after places that race cars have been driven round. They are drawn by hand on a grid about eighty cells wide, which is nowhere near enough to reproduce a real circuit — a hairpin comes out as a single cell. What they are after is the character of the place: Monza fast and barely interrupted, Monaco all corners. Treat them as sketches, not as maps.

Players

One lap, everybody on the line together.

How it works
  • Your car has a position and a velocity. Each turn you change the velocity by −1, 0 or +1 in each direction — nine choices — and the car then moves by the whole new velocity.
  • Neither part of the velocity may go outside −5…5.
  • Work out where that puts you. The game will not show you: adding the velocity to the position is the part worth practising. The pad tells you what the velocity becomes, and nothing else.
  • The whole line from where you are to where you land has to stay on the track. At speed 5 that line is five units long, and it can cut the corner out of a bend and back in again with both its ends still on the track. That counts as leaving the track, because it is.
  • Go off and the car is put back on the last whole-number point it reached with its speed set to zero, which costs you the turn. The game will show you exactly where the line left the track before it moves the car back.
  • A move that is genuinely impossible — another car standing exactly on the line, or over the speed limit — is crossed out and cannot be taken.
  • One lap. It counts when you cross the line the way the arrows point, having passed every hidden checkpoint in order along the way. You cannot see the checkpoints; following the arrows round is enough to collect them.

The car stands on a corner of the paper — a crossing of two lines — not in a square. The edge of the track is a curve that pays the squares no attention: it is drawn as a thin line exactly where the mathematics puts it, so what you see is what the game tests against.

Taking a turn: press one of the nine buttons and let go to take that move. Sliding off the button before letting go cancels it. Drag the track to move around it, pinch or scroll to zoom.

Install it: on Android, Chrome's ⋮ menu → “Add to Home screen”. On iOS, the share button in Safari's toolbar → scroll down → “Add to Home Screen”. Load the game once with a network connection and it works offline afterwards.
